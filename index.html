Advancing Quantum Technologies Through Nanophotonics and Integrated Photonics

I am a PhD candidate in Electrical Engineering at North Carolina State University, specializing in nanophotonics, integrated photonics, and quantum information science. My research focuses on designing and optimizing advanced photonic devices and integrated circuits to address challenges in quantum sensing, quantum communication, and quantum computing. My goal is to make quantum technologies more compact, efficient, robust, and scalable by combining electromagnetic modeling, computational optimization, nanofabrication, and experimental implementation of photonic devices and integrated circuits.

My research spans three interconnected areas: high-efficiency diffraction gratings and metasurfaces for miniaturized cold-atom systems used in quantum sensing, atomic clocks, and neutral-atom quantum computing; inverse-designed nanophotonic cavities for efficient spin–photon interfaces in quantum networks and photonic quantum computing; and programmable photonic processors for quantum information processing. My recent work includes the experimental realization of quantum signal processing on a room-temperature photonic integrated circuit, demonstrating programmable nonlinear function synthesis using single photons. Building on these results, I aim to advance integrated photonic platforms for quantum sensing and information processing.

Research Areas

Nanophotonics for Miniaturized Cold-Atom Systems and Quantum Sensing

GMOT gratings, metasurfaces, electromagnetic design, nanofabrication, optical integration, and experimental characterization.

Quantum Light & Nanophotonic Interfaces

High-Q nanophotonic cavities, quantum dots, efficient single-photon generation, and spin–photon interfaces.

Photonic Quantum Computing

Programmable photonic integrated circuits, experimental quantum signal processing, and hardware-native implementation of quantum algorithms.
